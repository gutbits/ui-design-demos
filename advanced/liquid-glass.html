Live Approximation

Drag the panel across the gradient. Toggle the SVG refraction filter on to see the backdrop visibly distort through the glass — the closest CSS gets to true optical refraction without WebGL.

DESIGN

Liquid Glass

Refractive, translucent, alive. The surface beneath shows through — bent, brightened, and tinted.

Honesty check: Apple's real Liquid Glass runs per-pixel ray refraction on the GPU (Metal), continuously sampling and bending live content at 120 Hz. The browser cannot do that with CSS alone. What you see here is an approximation: backdrop-filter for the frosted base + an SVG feDisplacementMap driven by feTurbulence to physically push the backdrop pixels, plus specular highlights faked with box-shadow and a moving gradient. For true real-time refraction you need WebGL/WebGPU shaders (see the Shaders page).

Anatomy of the Effect

Liquid Glass is layered. Each CSS/SVG technique contributes one optical property of real glass.

/* Layer 1 — the frosted base (this is classic glassmorphism) */ .glass { backdrop-filter: blur(3px) saturate(170%) brightness(1.08); background: linear-gradient(135deg, rgba(255,255,255,.18), rgba(255,255,255,.05)); /* Layer 3 — specular rim & inner bevel = the "wet" glassy edge */ border: 1px solid rgba(255,255,255,.35); box-shadow: inset 0 1px 1px rgba(255,255,255,.55), /* top highlight */ inset 0 -8px 24px rgba(255,255,255,.12), /* base glow */ 0 18px 50px rgba(0,0,0,.35); /* cast shadow */ } /* Layer 2 — REFRACTION: chain the SVG filter into backdrop-filter */ .glass.refract { backdrop-filter: blur(3px) saturate(170%) url(#liquidGlass); }
<!-- The SVG filter doing the optical bending --> <filter id="liquidGlass"> <!-- 1. organic noise field --> <feTurbulence type="fractalNoise" baseFrequency="0.008 0.012" numOctaves="2" result="noise"/> <!-- 2. soften so it flows like liquid --> <feGaussianBlur in="noise" stdDeviation="2" result="softNoise"/> <!-- 3. shove backdrop pixels along the noise gradient = refraction --> <feDisplacementMap in="SourceGraphic" in2="softNoise" scale="28" xChannelSelector="R" yChannelSelector="G"/> </filter>
  • feTurbulence — Perlin/fractal noise that becomes the "shape" of the glass surface. Lower baseFrequency = larger, gentler ripples.
  • feDisplacementMap — reads the noise's R & G channels and offsets each backdrop pixel by that amount. This is what literally bends the content beneath, mimicking how light refracts through a curved lens.
  • backdrop-filter — samples everything painted behind the element so the blur/distortion applies to live content, not a static image.
  • box-shadow insets — recreate the specular rim and the bright bottom "meniscus" you see on a real droplet of glass.
Browser support: backdrop-filter is broadly supported (Safari, Chrome, Firefox). Chaining an SVG url(#id) filter into backdrop-filter works in Chromium and Firefox but is inconsistent in Safari — always provide the plain-blur fallback shown in Layer 1 so the component degrades to standard glassmorphism.

Glassmorphism vs. Liquid Glass

Liquid Glass is the evolution of glassmorphism. Same translucent DNA — but it adds real-time distortion, dynamic specular light, and context-aware tint that samples the colors behind it.

Glassmorphism (2020) — frost only
2020

Frosted

Uniform blur. The backdrop is softened but never bent. Static, flat, calm.

Liquid Glass (2025) — refractive
2025

Refractive

Backdrop is bent and brightened. Edges catch light. The material feels physical and wet.

Lineage

Liquid Glass didn't appear from nowhere — it's the latest step in Apple's long arc between depth and flatness.

2007–2012

Aqua & Skeuomorphism

Glossy, light-reflecting "lickable" buttons. Real-world textures: leather, felt, linen. Heavy gradients and depth.

2013 · iOS 7

Flat + Translucency

Jony Ive strips the skeuomorphism but keeps frosted, blurred translucent panels (Control Center, dock). The seed of glass.

2020

Glassmorphism

The community names the frosted-glass trend; backdrop-filter: blur() ships in browsers. Big Sur leans into vibrancy.

2025 · WWDC

Liquid Glass

A unified material across iOS 26, iPadOS, macOS, watchOS & visionOS. GPU refraction, dynamic light, adaptive tint, fluid morphing.

Design intent: Apple frames Liquid Glass as content-first — the material is meant to recede, letting the UI float as a thin lens over your content rather than competing with it. Controls feel like physical glass that flexes, refracts and settles, reinforcing direct-manipulation and spatial continuity (especially important for visionOS depth).

When to Reach for Glass

  • Floating, transient surfaces — toolbars, sheets, notifications, HUDs that hover over rich content.
  • Over colorful or photographic backdrops — glass needs something interesting behind it to refract; over a flat gray it reads as muddy noise.
  • Sparingly — translucency hurts text contrast. Always add a tint layer + text-shadow and verify WCAG contrast. Don't stack glass on glass.
  • Mind performance — backdrop-filter and SVG displacement are GPU-expensive on low-end devices. Reduce blur radius and disable refraction on prefers-reduced-motion or weak hardware.
