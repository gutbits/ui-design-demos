💡 Pairing Rule of Thumb: Pick one font to be the "star" (display/headline) and one to be the "workhorse" (body). They should contrast in style but agree in mood. Don't use more than 2–3 fonts per project.

Typographic Scale

A typographic scale creates visual rhythm by using font sizes that relate mathematically. Choose a ratio and a base size — the scale generates all your headings.

Line Height & Letter Spacing

Line height (leading) and letter spacing (tracking) dramatically affect readability. Adjust the sliders to see the difference in real time.

Typography is the craft of endowing human language with a durable visual form. In the digital age, it's the primary material of user interfaces. When text is set well, readers don't notice the typography — they simply absorb the content. When it's set poorly, everything feels off. Good typography is invisible; bad typography is everywhere.


Pay special attention to line height for body text — too tight and lines blend together; too loose and the text floats apart. For body copy, 1.5–1.7 is the sweet spot. Headings can be tighter (1.1–1.3) because they're read differently.

Why White Space Is Not Wasted Space

Empty space isn't empty — it's a powerful design tool that lets content breathe and helps users focus on what matters.

In the early days of web design, every pixel was precious. Designers crammed content into every corner, fearing that white space meant lost opportunity. But research has shown the opposite: generous spacing improves comprehension by up to 20% and makes interfaces feel more premium and trustworthy.

White space creates visual hierarchy. It separates unrelated elements and groups related ones. It gives the eye a rest between sections. Masters of design — from Dieter Rams to Apple's Jony Ive — have always known: less is more.

Published on June 16, 2026 · 4 min read · Design, UX

💡 Hierarchy is built with: (1) Size — larger = more important. (2) Weight — bolder = more emphatic. (3) Color — brighter/saturated = higher priority; muted = supporting. (4) Spacing — more space above = new section. (5) Position — top-left is read first in LTR languages.
