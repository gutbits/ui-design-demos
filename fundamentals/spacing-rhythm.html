Line Height & Readability

Line height (leading) is the vertical space between lines. Too tight and lines collide; too loose and the eye loses the thread returning to the next line. For body text, 1.5–1.7 is the sweet spot.

line-height: 1.1 — too tight
Good typography is invisible. When leading is this tight, descenders nearly touch the next line and reading becomes tiring almost immediately.
line-height: 1.6 — just right
Good typography is invisible. With comfortable leading the eye glides from line to line, and a wall of text suddenly feels effortless to read.
line-height: 2.4 — too loose
Good typography is invisible. When leading is this loose the lines drift apart and the paragraph stops reading as a single connected block of text.
Rule of thumb: the longer the line of text, the more line height it needs. Headings (short lines) can go as tight as 1.1–1.3; long-form body copy benefits from 1.6+.

Vertical Rhythm & the Baseline Grid

When every element's height and margin is a multiple of one base unit (here, 28px), text and components snap to a shared vertical grid. This invisible regularity is what makes a page feel composed rather than assembled.

Rhythm you can feel

Vertical rhythm means spacing the elements of a page at consistent intervals derived from your base line-height. When headings, paragraphs, and images all align to the same baseline grid, the result is a subtle visual harmony.

Toggle the grid above. Notice how each line of text sits on a purple guide and the gaps between blocks are whole multiples of the base unit — never a stray 13px or 19px to break the cadence.

How to set it up: choose a base unit (commonly your body line-height, e.g. 28px) and make every vertical margin a multiple of it. Even approximate adherence dramatically improves perceived quality.
